# Style Confirmation

Use this file after `Purchase Decision Type`, `Page Strategy`, and `Best Hero Direction`, and before the final `Module Plan` and `Page Task Table`.

## Confirmation Gate

Do not finalize the module plan or generate the final ecommerce image set until the user approves a style direction. Present three style cards and three independent visual previews. The user may select one, combine selected traits, request adjustments, or request three new directions.

## Shared Comparison Conditions

Keep the comparison fair. All three previews must use:

- the same user product and product-identity reference;
- the same or closely matched product angle and product scale;
- the same core buyer demand and selling point;
- the same short headline and selling-point phrase;
- the same 3:4 portrait preview format, normally 1200×1600px;
- approximately the same information amount.

Change only the intended style variables: palette, background system, lighting, typography feel, composition language, information-card language, decorative devices, and scene atmosphere.

Style previews are confirmation samples, not final main images or detail-page deliverables.

## Style Card Fields

For each direction provide:

- direction name;
- three to five visual keywords;
- main, secondary, accent, and dark-anchor colors;
- background system;
- typography feel and title hierarchy;
- lighting system;
- composition characteristics and information density;
- category-fit visual devices;
- why it suits the current product and buyer demand;
- a short risk or tradeoff note.

Keep detailed explanation outside the generated preview image. Use only one short headline and one short selling-point phrase inside the preview to reduce text errors.

## No Style Reference Provided

Create three clearly different, category-appropriate directions from the product type, buyer demand, purchase decision type, and best hero direction. Do not offer three directions that differ only by color.

## Style Reference Provided

First analyze the reference for palette, background, lighting, composition, typography feel, information density, decorative language, and product scale. If multiple references are provided, state which reference controls which style dimension and separate incompatible references into distinct groups.

Treat product images and style references as different input roles:

- product images lock product identity, structure, color, material, proportions, and visible details;
- style references guide palette, background, lighting, composition rhythm, typography feel, and atmosphere only.

Never copy or transfer the reference's product, brand, logo, packaging, copy, trademark, or distinctive brand-owned layout into the user's product.

Create these three variants:

1. `A — High-reference adaptation`: stay closest to the reference's palette, lighting, atmosphere, and rhythm without copying exact layout or brand assets.
2. `B — Optimized-reference adaptation`: retain the strongest reference traits while improving product clarity, selling-point hierarchy, readability, scene logic, and ecommerce conversion. Mark this as recommended by default.
3. `C — Extended-reference adaptation`: retain the reference's core mood while redesigning composition, scene, title zone, and visual devices more freely.

## User Selection

Offer these actions in the user's language:

- choose A, B, or C;
- choose one direction with specific adjustments;
- combine named traits from two directions;
- reject all three and request new directions.

After approval, record the chosen direction and every requested adjustment in `最终风格确认.md`. Convert the approved result into `Campaign Style Lock`, `Style System Lock`, and `Design Strength Lock`, then continue to module planning.
